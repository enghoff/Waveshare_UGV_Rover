# M5 control 1: a 15-minute run over the whole flat reported no furniture-sized change

**The first of M5's controls, an ordinary run with nothing moved, logged no
change of furniture size.** `run/710476ae/1` started from the charger at 08:04 on
2026-10-11, unfenced, and ran its full 15 minutes: 74.6 m, 85 actions, 32
drives, 27 arrived and 5 blocked, none failed for any other reason. The change
watch ([ros_nav/README.md](../../ros_nav/README.md), *What has changed since a
place was last seen*) counted the whole run, refused 3 scans as not fitting what
it knew, and logged 14 small changes, all under 0.6 m across. It is one control
of the ten or more criterion 1 of [M5](../plans/autonomous-curiosity.md) asks
for, and on its own says only that false alarms are not the usual outcome of a
run.

## What was unchanged, and what was not

Before the run the owner said nothing had moved since about 15:00 the day
before, when session 20 last saw most of the flat, except four doors, now
closed, which the rover should not try to pass. They marked them on the
console's map; placed on the occupancy grid (an 87% match of the walls), they
lie between 2 and 6 m from the charger, along the hallway the rover drives.
The rover passed within 0.4-0.6 m of each. Two of the five blocked drives
stopped beside one, at 1.0 m and 0.7 m: goals behind a closed door, probably,
which the rover gave up on as blocked rather than pushing at. Two of the small
changes sit on two of the doors (NEW, 0.45 and 0.30 m across): a closed door is
a short stretch of new wall across a doorway, under the 0.6 m the watch calls
furniture, as the doors on the 2026-10-10 recordings were.

The other twelve small changes are along walls and furniture edges, most of them
in the charger room against the evening before, and one of them was logged
twice: the watch logs a group again when it drops out of one report and comes
back in the next. They are not counted as alarms, since nothing smaller than
furniture is.

## Counted how

`experiments/m5_changes/control_count.py` lists what the watch appended to its
log between the run's opening and the next report after it ended. The run was
recorded (`recordings/bags/m5-control1-080421` on the rover, 33 MB) for replay
when the watch changes.

The run ended on its time with a drive under way; the rover was driven back to
the charger and parked 14 degrees off the asked heading, as arrivals do. Read
off the recording, the parked scan fitted the map at 98% at the heading
navigation held, and the restart that followed for the deploy below restored
the same pose.

## A fault found on the way: the clock after a cold boot

The watch's report at 07:56 dated the visit it compared against 95.8 s after
1970. After the power has been off, the rover's clock reads 1970 until the
network sets it, about a minute after boot, and the watch had counted that
minute's scans into the visit it had saved the night before -- not "more than
300 s later" but 56 years earlier -- then closed that visit with the 1970 time
on it. The comparison itself was barely touched, since the minute was the same
parked view, but the date was wrong. Reproduced in `test_change_watch` (the
reference dated 79.7 s), fixed in 9ef8f69: a scan dated more than a minute before
the newest the watch holds is set aside and counted in its report's
`before_clock_set`. Deployed at 08:22, the watch's 30 checks pass on the rover
and it is counting; it has not yet met a cold boot.
