## A drive refused for a move nobody had made

Session 17's first goal, sent 34 s after the rover had arrived in the bedroom,
was refused as "a move is already running" when nothing was moving: my drive
had ended, the other agent working here had sent the rover nothing, and
navigation logged no move. The executive recorded the refusal and went on to
its next goal, as criterion 7 asks. It is the only such refusal in the 71 runs
the executive's log holds.

The cause is navigation's drift check, which matches a scan against the whole
map every five minutes to say whether the rover is where it thinks. It held the
lock that every drive, turn and goal takes for the whole of its search, about a
second, so a goal arriving then was turned away. The check only reads, and it
takes its scan and pose as it starts, so it now skips when a move is running
without taking the lock itself, as the world state's own measurement already
did (4e3a447). `test_maprestore.py` sends a drive half way through a check:
refused before the change, given the wheels after. It was deployed at 14:07,
after the sessions, with the daemon's battery logger (a435582). On the rover the
test passes against the deployed code, as do the rest of its 1175, and the
check still runs: right after the restart it reported the scan against the
map. The race itself, a goal arriving in that second, was not produced on the
rover; it had come once in 71 runs.

## A restore that believed a stale pose

After a restart at noon the map was unsettled, and the rover was driven from
the bedroom to the charger before anybody refitted it. The pose on disk is
saved only while the map is settled, so it stayed the bedroom's. At the next
reboot, on the charger, the restore anchored at that saved pose, found the
mapper's anchor near it, and called the map settled: "the rover is where it was
parked", 9.5 m from where it stood. Only 58% of the scan lay on a wall there. A
whole-flat search for the rover's place found several perfect fits and could
not choose, so the owner carried the rover to the bedroom pose and refitted it.

A mapper that can match nothing also stays near the pose it was given, so
agreeing with it proved nothing. The first look at the lidar after a restore
now takes the claim back when less than 75% of the scan lies on a wall at the
restored pose and the scan's own fit does not agree (6741cf5). The map then
stays unsettled, runs are refused, and its note asks for a refit. It only ever
withdraws a claim, so [R-NAV-3](../requirements/navigation.md#r-nav-3) holds.
`test_maprestore.py` reproduces the 58% case and keeps the 85% and 98% ones
settled. On the rover it was deployed at 12:57 and restarted at the charger
with the armchair moved, and the map stayed settled at about 85%. The failing
case itself has not recurred on the rover since.

At 13:06, after a brownout, the restore was unsettled for the ordinary reason:
the run had moved the rover before it switched off. The lidar's fit agreed with
where the mapper put it, to 4 cm, and I pressed the refit, at the owner's word
to keep going. It was a confirming refit and moved nothing.

## Brownouts under load

The rover switched itself off seconds after the first run of session 15 began
at 13:02, on a battery that had read 80% at rest after a few minutes' charge,
and 20% at rest after the reboot. The same happened on 2026-10-09 at 18:37,
seconds after a drive at "25% recovering". Why is not known, and the pack's
voltage alone does not explain it: session 16 drove for 15 minutes at 9.3-9.6 V
under load without switching off, and session 19 ran on until about 8.9 V by
the owner's reading ([the battery reading](2026-10-10-battery-reading-hides-half-the-pack.md)).
Both brownouts came at far higher voltages than that. A voltage logger in the
daemon now keeps the lowest voltage of every five seconds on disk, so the next
one will show what the pack did just before it.
The owner accepts a brownout as the cost of driving to the end of a charge.
