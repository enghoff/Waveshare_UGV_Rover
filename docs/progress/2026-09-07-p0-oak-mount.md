# Where the OAK actually sits, and the arithmetic error that hid it

The fixed OAK-to-gimbal mount is measured and adopted. It took a correction to
the bench's own pose fit to get there: the fit was stopping short of the answer
it was supposed to compute, and that alone was worth 1.5 degrees of board tilt
per frame. With it corrected, both the development set and the held-out set at a
second distance pass every gate that was declared before either was captured, and
`world_state/oak.py` now carries a measured offset as well as a measured rotation
for the first time.

The honest caveat, stated once here and repeated in the code: the held-out set
was **re-analysed** after the fit was corrected. It was not re-captured. The
images and the acceptance rules are the ones from before, and nothing about the
correction was chosen by looking at whether it made the gate pass — but a reader
who wants a clean second trial has not had one, and the way to get it is in the
last section.

## The question

The [M0 review](2026-09-07-m0-review.md) left the mount inconclusive. Twenty-five
pair estimates from five gimbal frames and five OAK frames of one stationary board
spanned 0.863 degrees of yaw, against a 0.75-degree repeatability limit declared
before capture. The question was which of the three candidates — one bad gimbal
frame, one bad OAK frame, or the pairing arithmetic — produced that spread.

## What the evidence was

Everything below comes from images already on disk. No hardware was touched and
nothing was re-captured.

- `captures/p0-gimbal-2026-09-07/oak-mount-held-out-02/` — five gimbal frames at
  1280 x 960 and five OAK frames at 1920 x 1080, board at a median 0.685 m from
  the gimbal camera.
- `captures/p0-gimbal-2026-09-07/oak-mount-dev/` — the development set, same
  board, 0.555 m, OAK at 640 x 360.
- `captures/p0-gimbal-2026-09-07/held-out-01/analysis.json` — the gimbal camera's
  own calibration, which supplies the fisheye model both sets are solved through.

## It was the gimbal frames, and then it was not the frames at all

Factoring the 25 pairs as a two-way table separates the two inputs cleanly,
because the pairing is very nearly additive: the interaction term is 0.0% of the
variance in every one of the six components. The gimbal frames carry **99.2% of
the yaw variance** and the OAK frames 0.8%. Had the five gimbal frames been
identical, the yaw spread would have been 0.068 degrees rather than 0.863. The
same holds for every other component, gimbal between 96% and 99%.

That is not a bad frame. All five gimbal frames found 50 to 54 corners, all
reprojected within 0.234 px, and their detected corner positions moved only
0.137 px from frame to frame — the OAK's moved 0.103 px, barely less. Five still
frames of a still board nevertheless disagreed about the board's orientation by
0.789 degrees, where the OAK's five disagreed by 0.068.

The cause is the pose fit. The bench solved the board with `SOLVEPNP_IPPE`, which
is an analytic planar solution rather than a minimiser, and on a board that is
small in frame and close to face-on it stops well short of the least-squares
optimum:

| | reprojection | frame-to-frame orientation disagreement |
|---|---:|---:|
| IPPE, as the bench had it | 0.224-0.234 px | 0.789 deg |
| IPPE then refined by Levenberg-Marquardt | 0.179-0.201 px | 0.361 deg |
| SQPNP | — | 0.361 deg |
| Iterative | — | 0.360 deg |

Three solvers that minimise agree with each other to 0.001 degrees and disagree
with the one that does not. Refinement lowered the residual on **every one of the
twenty frames** in the two sets, and moved each fitted pose by about 1.5 degrees,
consistently about the camera's horizontal axis.

The residual pattern says the same thing without any appeal to authority. Laid
out on the board grid, the IPPE residuals run positive along the top row and
negative along the bottom — a coherent shear that a small change of board tilt
absorbs. After refinement the trend is gone and what is left looks like
detection noise. A physically bowed board would leave a smooth bulge that no pose
change could remove; this was simply an unconverged fit.

**Simulation could not reproduce it, and the hardware is right.** Projecting the
board synthetically through the same fisheye model and adding 0.05 to 0.30 px of
corner noise makes refinement worth 0.3-0.5% of residual and about 0.1 degrees of
pose — two orders of magnitude less than the real frames show. Injecting lens-model
error or a radial systematic did not close the gap either. The synthetic model is
recorded here as having failed to predict the effect, not as supporting it.

## What the corrected fit says

Both sets were re-analysed with refinement on both cameras. Every gate passes.

| Component | Development | Held-out | Change | Declared limit |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Yaw | +1.4923 deg | +1.7627 deg | +0.2704 | 0.75 |
| Pitch | +6.2555 deg | +6.2300 deg | -0.0255 | 0.75 |
| Roll | -1.2000 deg | -1.2334 deg | -0.0333 | 0.75 |
| Forward | +0.0872 m | +0.0996 m | +0.0124 | 0.015 |
| Left | -0.0031 m | -0.0008 m | +0.0023 | 0.015 |
| Up | -0.0937 m | -0.0940 m | -0.0004 | 0.015 |

Repeatability within each set, which is what failed before: development yaw range
0.137 degrees and held-out 0.357, both against the unchanged 0.75 limit. The
worst offset range is 3.6 mm against 15 mm. Target distance separation was
0.130 m against a 0.10 m minimum. The gimbal frames spanned 50-54 corners and the
OAK frames 54, against gates of 40 and 45.

The old numbers, for the record: development yaw +0.1155 and pitch +7.4883,
held-out yaw +0.8254 and pitch +7.9397, with a held-out yaw range of 0.8634. The
pitch was out by more than a degree and the disagreement between the two distances
was 0.451 degrees of pitch where it is now 0.026.

**Two independent distances agreeing is the load-bearing evidence.** Refinement
was chosen on residual and solver agreement, both of which are internal to a
single set. That the corrected transform then agrees between a 0.555 m capture and
a 0.685 m capture — pitch to 0.026 degrees, roll to 0.033, height to 0.4 mm, where
before it agreed to 0.451, 0.070 and 7.7 mm — is a check the correction could not
have been tuned to pass.

## What is still wrong, in the result that passed

The forward offset moved **12.4 mm between the two distances** while repeating to
under half a millimetre within each of them. That is thirty times the within-set
spread, so it is systematic rather than noise. It survives every corner-detection
path the bench can take — native, three-times enlarged, or whichever finds more
corners — changing by less than 0.1 mm across all three, so it is not a detection
artefact. It is inside the 15 mm the acceptance rule allowed, but only just, and
no mechanism for it has been established. A camera focal error large enough to
explain it would have to be about 10%, which is not credible for either lens, so
that is not the explanation.

Read the adopted forward offset as good to about a centimetre. At two metres a
centimetre is worth roughly a third of a degree of bearing, against the 1.5
degrees the resolver is told to expect, so it is comfortably affordable — but it
is not the sub-millimetre figure the within-set repeatability would suggest.

A smaller loose end: in the development set four gimbal frames were detected at
native resolution and one at three-times enlargement, because the bench takes
whichever finds more corners per frame. Forcing one path throughout moves the
development yaw by up to 0.10 degrees. The held-out set used the enlarged path for
all five frames and is unaffected.

## What changed in the repository

- `usb_cameras/calibrate_gimbal.py` and `usb_cameras/calibrate_oak_mount.py`
  refine every candidate pose to the least-squares optimum before choosing
  between the planar solution's two branches.
- `usb_cameras/calibrate_oak_mount.py` gained a `pose_converged` gate. It nudges
  each fitted pose by a tenth of a degree about each axis and fails if that
  recovers more than 0.5% of the residual. On the old fit it would have recovered
  1.4-4.4% on all ten gimbal frames; it now recovers 0.00% on all twenty. This is
  a new mechanical check, not a relaxation of any existing one.
- `world_state/oak.py` carries the adopted transform and a rewritten account of
  where it came from. The superseded `bench_oak.py` narrative — the ORB feature
  match whose answer moved 4.5 degrees depending on where the gimbal was pointed,
  and the ruler reading of 40 mm forward and 110 mm below — is summarised there
  and preserved here rather than in the constant's comments.

The adopted value is the **development** candidate, as the protocol declared
before capture, with the held-out set as its confirmation. The two were not
averaged.

## Numbers taken again after the change

- Gimbal placement, `held-out-01`, re-analysed: ascending gain error -0.34%
  (was -0.535%), absolute residual p95 0.297 degrees (was 0.457), stationary
  duplicate difference median 0.074 and p95 0.225 degrees (were 0.117 and 0.423).
  Still passes its `consistent-ascending` candidate rule, with more margin. The
  operating envelope is unchanged: commanded pan -20 to +20 at tilt zero, reached
  from the ascending direction.
- `campaign-01` remains inconclusive and `campaign-02` remains a development
  measurement. No verdict changed direction.
- The fisheye intrinsics did not change at all, since fitting them never used the
  pose solver: 0.173 px RMS, fx 542.68.
- `world_state/selftest.py` on this workstation: 748 passed, 0 failed, 0 skipped.

## What the change makes stale, which is the owner's decision

Adopting a new mount divides the store in two, and nothing in it records which
side a row is on. Counted on the rover after the deploy:

- **838 observations carry a range taken through the old mount**, the last of them
  at 15:19:50 on 2026-09-07 (observation 33777). Three carry one taken through the
  new mount, from observation 33784.
- Every one of those rows was written by the *gimbal* camera, so their bearings are
  unaffected — a gimbal bearing never passes through this constant. What the mount
  decided was **where in the depth picture to sample**, so it is the ranges alone
  that are in question.
- All 131 placed entities were fitted from that mixture, in map session 67.

The old mount was about three degrees out in both yaw and pitch, which at two
metres is roughly ten centimetres across the depth image — enough to take the
range off a neighbouring surface rather than the thing in the box. That is the
same failure the [M0 baseline](2026-09-07-m0-semantic-world-state.md) already
described from the other end, where only 48% of ranges landed within half a metre
of an independent bearing crossing.

The store has no column saying which calibration a range came from. Its schema
anticipates the need — the `camera` column exists so a row "can be re-examined
when one of the two turns out to have been mounted crooked" — but a mount that
changes underneath a fixed camera has no equivalent stamp. **Nothing has been
deleted or invalidated here**, because how to treat the old ranges is a decision
about the owner's data and about what R-WS-5's map-session rule ought to become
for calibration, not a detail of this measurement. The three candidates are to
stamp the calibration on each row from now on and ignore rows without one, to drop
the 838 ranges and keep their bearings, or to clear the semantic world and rebuild
it. The depth-alignment check in the next section cannot be trusted until one of
them is chosen, because it would otherwise average two calibrations together.

## Requirements

- [R-WS-11](../requirements/world-state.md#r-ws-11) stays `open`, but half of what
  blocked it is now measured. The OAK-to-gimbal translation is known to about a
  centimetre; the gimbal camera's offset from the SLAM pose is still unmeasured,
  and absolute height above the floor needs both.
- [R-WS-10](../requirements/world-state.md#r-ws-10) stays `failing`. This entry
  does not touch it. The mount is measured against a common object and never
  through the gimbal's pointing, so the roughly 1.5 degrees of backlash and the
  four-to-eight-per-cent gain walk are exactly where they were, and they are what
  R-WS-10 is about.
- No other requirement moved.

## Next, in order

1. **Capture a fresh held-out set to make the second trial clean.** Same
   protocol, a third distance at least 0.10 m from both 0.555 m and 0.685 m, with
   the corrected fit and the `pose_converged` gate in place from the start. This
   is the one thing outstanding that needs the owner and the printed target.
2. **Turn the board off face-on while doing it.** The whole difficulty here is
   that a nearly face-on planar target determines its own out-of-plane tilt
   poorly. Simulation puts the improvement from a 20-30 degree turn about the
   vertical at roughly three to four times, and unlike the effect above this is a
   prediction that the capture will test rather than a claim.
3. Check foreground and background depth-to-object alignment at working
   distances, now that the mount carries an offset.
4. Enforce the demonstrated ascending gimbal approach in the semantic capture
   path, and refuse captures taken outside the supported state.
5. The rest of M0: the [R-WS-16](../requirements/world-state.md#r-ws-16)
   confirmed-pose capture gate, background and floor eligibility, movement-eligible
   identity, and reviewed associations. Semantic movement stays disabled while
   those are open.
