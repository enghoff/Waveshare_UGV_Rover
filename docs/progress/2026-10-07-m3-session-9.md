# M3 session 9: looks without depth are harmless; at a third of a charge the rover cannot pivot

**Run `run/58d585a2/4` drove the charger room, fenced, for about 3 minutes with
the depth camera's service stopped.** It took 15 goals, 7.2 m and 29 actions.
All 13 looks completed without depth, and the run went on as normal. It ended
when the rover could no longer turn on the spot, and I stopped it.
Commanded at full turning power, it barely rotated, with the battery at 10.7 V
under load and 11.3 V (30%) once standing. There was no contact, and the rover
stayed inside the safe area. The depth service was restarted afterwards and
answered "on".

## What happened

- **Before opening**, the depth service was stopped the way its runner documents
  (`pkill -f run_oak_depth.sh`, then the server). The daemon's depth calls
  answered "connection refused", which is what a camera gone from USB leaves.
- **13 looks, none failed and none improved its thing.** The run did not notice
  the depth camera was gone. It went on choosing geometry goals whose predicted
  gain partly assumes a range they could not get.
- **The end.** A near goal 1.1 m away had to be reached facing -64 degrees, so
  the rover turned to face it first (-156 degrees). In its 10.5 s allowance the
  heading did not move. The goal fell back to a single Nav2 goal, which pivoted
  at PWM 101/-101 while turning a few degrees, tried 3 recoveries and timed out
  after 45 s. The live alert reported "driving 20 s, moved 0.01 m"; the next
  drive's turn had just begun when I stopped the run. The stall watch had
  rightly left the goal alone, because Nav2 was attempting recoveries.

## What it means

- **The depth-unavailable condition passes as a safety matter.** Looks that
  cannot range neither fail nor stop the run. What it costs is battery: a run
  without depth spends looks valued partly for depth. Whether the run should
  check depth before choosing a geometry goal is a scoring question for later,
  not a safety fault.
- **Turning on the spot gives out before driving straight does.** At 11.3 V
  standing this tracked chassis could not pivot under its own drive. The two
  turns that ran out of time in [session 8](2026-10-07-m3-session-8.md) may share
  the cause, though one was beside an obstacle. Since 2026-10-07 a near goal with
  a heading begins with a pivot, so a low battery now shows up as failed drives
  rather than a stopped rover. The owner's 10% floor is read in percent, and the
  percent reading moved between 5 and 55 within minutes during this window,
  while volts stayed consistent. A floor for supervised drives is better set in
  volts at rest, at the point where pivots stop working. That point is not
  measured yet: somewhere above 11.3 V.
