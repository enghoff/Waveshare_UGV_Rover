# M0 review: bounded calibration and usable operating limits

Status: P0 is in progress; M0 has not passed. Independent gimbal development and
held-out measurements are complete, and the first OAK-mount preflight is
inconclusive because the OAK could see only part of the reference. No runtime
calibration has been changed. The current protocol and acceptance gate are in
[the autonomy plan](../plans/autonomous-curiosity.md#phase-0----close-the-semantic-world-state-prerequisite).

recording, reviewed associations and subsequent camera-to-camera bench findings.
Its original criterion counts describe the gate used at that time; they do not

- The gain-like slope was reported as 3.7-7.6%, with drift limiting precision.
  A single 7% correction is not established. Pan-dependent roll remains observed,
  with lens/axis/servo contributions not independently separated.
- Lower-appearance-score wrong associations and floor patches treated as objects

- The separately recorded [refit investigation](2026-09-07-refit-window.md) reports

## Independent measurements completed

The first 640 x 480 development attempt was retained as inconclusive: its stationary
duplicate difference had a 0.652-degree median and 2.910-degree 95th percentile.
That reproduced a measurement-resolution limit in the camera path rather than a
servo result. The daemon's snapshot call was then extended to the camera's supported
1280 x 960 mode. The deployed call returned and decoded a 1280 x 960 image with 48
ChArUco corners; the daemon suite passed 841/0/0 on the Orin after deployment.

The 1280 x 960 development campaign fitted the fisheye at 0.171 px RMS and measured
pan gain error of +0.04%. It found a -1.87-degree approach-direction offset and a
stationary duplicate difference of 0.192 degrees median, 0.358 degrees p95. This
withdraws the earlier 4-8% gain estimate as a calibration artefact while confirming
the physical direction-dependent effect.

The candidate was declared before the held-out run: keep gain unchanged and finish
each supported placement from the ascending direction. At held-out commanded angles
-20, -15, -5, +5, +15 and +20 degrees, the reference gate passed at 0.117 degrees
median and 0.423 degrees p95. The candidate's gain error was -0.535% and its absolute
residual p95 was 0.457 degrees, so it passed its predeclared 1.0% and 0.5-degree gates.
The opposite-direction gap remains physical and measured at 1.19-2.23 degrees across
the tested positions. The accepted gimbal envelope therefore requires the consistent
ascending final approach; it does not claim backlash has disappeared.

The first fixed-OAK mount preflight used five stationary frames from each camera.
Repeated fits were tight: yaw, pitch and roll ranges were 0.407, 0.559 and 0.070
degrees, while each offset component spanned at most 4.4 mm. This is repeatable but
not accepted. The OAK saw only 24 of 54 ChArUco corners: all six rows but only four
of nine columns, because the upper part of the portrait-mounted board is outside its
frame. With that partial plane, using the OAK's stored distortion rather than a
pinhole changes fitted pitch by 2.44 degrees despite both fits having about 0.2 px
reprojection residual. That is an observability failure which a repeatability or
reprojection check alone would miss.

The OAK service now publishes its exact stored colour-camera distortion in `/health`.
This was deployed and proved live on the Orin. Across a grid covering the emitted
640 x 360 frame, its existing pinhole runtime approximation differs from the stored
lens model by 0.467 degrees at p95 and 0.548 degrees maximum. That approximation is
inside the predeclared 0.75-degree mount-bench allowance; the partial-board pose is
the current blocker. The new `usb_cameras/calibrate_oak_mount.py` refuses a fit unless
the OAK has at least 45 corners, repeated angles and offsets meet their limits, and a
separate held-out placement agrees before adoption.

## Limits of the measurement chain

[The lens constants](../../face_tracking/lens.py) were derived from pan/tilt sweeps
using commanded angles. [The OAK bench](../../world_state/bench_oak.py) uses that
lens and reads back the angle commanded to the servo, not an independently sensed
shaft position. Repeating that fit can reveal disagreement and repeatability, but
cannot by itself establish a unique servo correction independent of lens error.

The configured 1.5-degree bearing uncertainty is an assumption to validate, not a
required mechanical accuracy. A separation between approach directions is neither
automatically a standard deviation nor the unavoidable error of a corrected system.
Measurement uncertainty, repeatable bias and remaining physical variation need
separate estimates before any uncertainty setting changes.

## Scope agreed following the review

No requirement changes state in this review. R-WS-10 remains failing; R-WS-11,
R-WS-12, R-WS-13 and R-WS-16 remain open. The software suite is not evidence that any of
these physical/acceptance requirements has been settled.

The owner explicitly asked that calibration respect the physical limitations of
the setup and not pursue unattainable precision. The agreed M0 interpretation is
adequate measured accuracy and honest uncertainty within declared useful limits,
with unsupported conditions refused. Perfect pointing and identifying every object
are not acceptance requirements.

The plan now specifies a bounded campaign, held-out task checks and exit decisions:
accept a useful demonstrated envelope, narrow it and retest, or stop with the
remaining limitation documented. The gimbal-placement sub-envelope has passed;
the complete camera/depth envelope has not, and no runtime calibration constant is
changed by this review.

The old baseline's "before Phase 1" heading is historical. Read-only M1 recording
and M2 shadow decisions may proceed alongside P0; semantic movement still waits for
M0 and the subsequent execution gates. The gimbal's -20 to +20 degree, tilt-zero
envelope has passed its measurement gate with a consistent ascending final approach.
The complete camera/depth operating envelope is not yet accepted.

The next owner action is to lower the flat target by about 120 mm without moving the
rover. That should bring all nine ChArUco columns into the lower OAK's frame while
remaining inside the gimbal camera's field. The operator then takes a full-board
development mount capture, repeats it at a second rover-to-target distance as the
held-out trial, and adopts a transform only if the predeclared agreement gates pass.
Foreground/background depth alignment, capture-path approach enforcement and the
R-WS-16 confirmed-pose gate remain after that.
