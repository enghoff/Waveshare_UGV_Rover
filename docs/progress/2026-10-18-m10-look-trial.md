**On 92 looks taken after it was frozen, the landing predictor's chances
scored a Brier of 0.064, against 0.102 for the history's share as a constant
and 0.177 for the planner's old rule, and every bin it put 20 looks or more in
landed within 0.07 of what it predicted.** That meets the declared criteria 1,
2, 3 and 5 ([the
decision](../decisions/m10-predicts-whether-a-look-lands.md)), and 6 is the
unit tests'. Criterion 4's offline comparison is met only as a tie: at 13 of the
16 stops the new ranking chose the same look as the old, and each ranking's
choice landed at 2 stops. That shows no improvement, and physical benefit was
never to be claimed from this trial. **M10 is not passed**: criterion 4 needs
supervised runs with and without the predictor.

**The trial also says looks land less often than the history did**: 6 of 92,
against 115 of 430. Most of the predictor's margin over the declared constant is
that it expected this (7.8% on average); a constant set in hindsight at the
trial's own rate would have scored 0.061, slightly better than the predictor.
Within the trial it ranks looks better than chance (AUC 0.72), on six landings.
[R-WS-17](../requirements/world-state.md#r-ws-17) stays proposed.

## The run

`m10trial1-1018`, `experiments/m10_outcomes/look_trial.py run`, launched
detached on the rover (`trial_launch.sh`) after a READY pre-flight at 93%. The
survey loop out to the bedroom and back, 17:07:19-17:19:12, battery 91% to 75%,
home 0.05 m from the spot. The position check agreed within 0.06 m at every
stop and 0.13 m at home.

One leg was refused: from the bedroom's first stop on the way back, the
corridor's second was "nowhere within half a metre where the rover's body fits",
the scan seeing something the map did not have. The script took that stop's
looks from where it stood, (-9.05, -12.23), each predicted from there, and drove
on to the next stop normally.

Up to six looks a stop, drawn at random among the records in reach (7 to 33 a
stop), each with its chance recorded before it was taken: 96 looks at 53
records. Every one carries the frozen predictor's digest, `088b4d51a9a9`, and
none fell back to the old half. Four came back with no picture (one refused
because the previous inspection was still running, three with no frame and no
error) and are not scored. The world model filed the trial's pictures by 17:29;
`landed.py` read the result.

The scored looks with their features and outcomes:
[m10trial1-1018-landed.json](../../experiments/m10_outcomes/trials/m10trial1-1018-landed.json).
The recording is `recordings/bags/session-m10trial1-1018` on the rover.

## Why so few landed

| | Looks | Predicted, mean | Landed |
|---|---|---|---|
| target seen under 3 h before | 33 | 0.124 | 3 |
| 3 to 24 h | 11 | 0.078 | 1 |
| over a day | 48 | 0.047 | 2 |
| a rival within 0.8 m | 80 | 0.057 | 4 |
| no rival | 12 | 0.219 | 2 |

80 of the 92 had a record at least as established within 0.8 m, and 84 had
regions of their picture filed to other records. Records seen recently landed
3 times in 33, against 41% in the history. The trial drew from every record in
reach rather than the ones a planner chose, which is likely why rivals dominate
here, but this is not established. Two of the six landings were one record in
the bedroom, v1012, unseen for 28 hours, the kind the history says almost never
lands.

## What it leaves

A supervised comparison of runs with and without the predictor is what
criterion 4 asks for, and at this landing rate it has little to measure: with
the predictor the planner finds almost no look worth its cost, and without it
about one look in fifteen lands. Making more looks land is the world state's
work (*Let looks reach their things* in [the
plan](../plans/autonomous-curiosity.md#what-comes-next)); the comparison means
more after it.
