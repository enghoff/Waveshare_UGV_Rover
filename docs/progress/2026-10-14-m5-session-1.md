# M5 session 1: five of seven staged events found, no false alarm, and step 3 of the what/where plan fails its test

**The first counted M5 session ran cleanly from start to finish. By M5's
rules it found five of the seven staged events: both armchairs' old and new
places, and the red bean bag. It missed the dining chair's departure and
arrival, and logged no false alarm in five room-controls.** The same session
was step 3's test in
[prediction in what and where streams](../plans/what-where-streams.md), fixed
before the session. Step 3 joins the change watch's geometry to the camera's
appearance. It found nothing the watch did not, and named one of its four
counted events right, so it failed.

## What ran

Session `session1-1014`, 2026-10-14 11:23-11:37, by
`experiments/m5_changes/session.py`, step by step, with the wait lengthened to
an hour so that the rover moved on only when the owner said the staging was
done. Loop B ran before the autonomous run, the reverse of the shakedown, as
the arms alternate.

## Step 3's test

`score3.py` replayed each arm from the watch state saved after the wait and
counted both methods by M5's rules (branch `what-where`, `step3.md`).

| | Watch alone | Step 3 |
|---|---|---|
| staged events found | 6 of 7 | the same 6 |
| found by one and not the other | | none |
| events named right | | 1 of 4 |
| false alarms in room-controls | 5 | 6 |

**Step 3 fails**: it finds nothing more, and names under four in five right.
Two of the scorer's rules inflated its counts. Neither changes the verdict:
- **Found:** each method's replay put the bean bag's arrival within 0.6 m of
  the chair's new place, so it counted for both. M5's own count from the live
  log, 0.64 m away, did not.
- **False alarms:** a run replayed on its own finds the staged changes again,
  in a living room M5 counts as a control by then. The live log had none.

**Why it named so little.** Naming is counted in loop B, the first arm after
the staging:
- **Not enough pictures.** On a loop the camera looks the way the rover drives.
  In loop B it saw the armchair by the cabinet once and the bean bag not at all.
  In the run, which looked about it, step 3 named both armchair moves.
- **The bean bag is not new to the house.** It was there for the shakedown two
  days earlier, so the camera, comparing with everything it remembers, called
  its arrival familiar.
- **The dining chair** looks like the other seven, and its departure was never
  seen.

## What this means

The lidar watch carries M5's detection on its own: five of seven, no false
alarm. Step 3 adds names only where the camera has looked at a change. Its
novelty test asks the wrong question for a thing brought back from another
room. Before it is tested again:
- the camera must be aimed at what the watch reports, rather than catching it
  in passing;
- "new" must mean new to this place, not to the house;
- the scorer must match staged events to changes one to one, and judge
  room-controls from a continuous replay.

That is development, and the next staged session is its next test. No
requirement moved.
