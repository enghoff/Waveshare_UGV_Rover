# M3 session 12: nobody stood in the way; the rover stuck on the rug under the table

**Four runs in the charger room, about 13 minutes and 33 m, from 60% down to
the owner's 10% floor and back to the origin.** The condition was again meant
to be a person blocking the rover. The owner did not block it, and the rover
never waited for anything in its way, so the condition was not tried and stays
open. What the session found instead was the rug under the dining table: the
rover drove onto it, pivoted at full turning power without turning, and the
run was stopped. Runs no longer choose a drive target on the rug (db852e4),
which went out between the second and third runs.

## What happened

- **First run**, `run/58d585a2/9`: 2.2 minutes, 5.3 m, 19 actions. The stuck
  alert fired at (-17.36, -12.12), on the rug: 20 s driving, 0.00 m moved,
  while a turn sat at full power. The run was stopped 20 s later, and the owner
  reported the rover stuck on the rug. One turn had already timed out in Nav2
  (code 701).
- **Second run**, `run/58d585a2/10`: about 9 minutes, 27 m, 87 actions. A
  second turn timed out, at a goal moved 46 cm to where the body fits. The floor
  tripped at 10.91 V.
- **Third and fourth runs**, `run/58d585a2/11` and `/12`: reopened each time a
  minute's rest put the reading back above 10%, half a minute each. The floor
  tripped again at 11.04 V, then 11.03 V standing. A person's drive took the
  rover back to the origin, ending 13 cm from it.

## What it means for M3

- **The person condition is still owed**, with the near-goal wait and go-round
  untested on the rover. Session 13 is for that.
- **The rug is a place the lidar cannot keep the rover off.** Its scan plane
  is about 20 cm up and a rug is flat. The owner asked for it not to be a drive
  target rather than a no-go zone, so navigation still crosses it. Whether
  crossing alone gets the rover stuck is something later sessions will show.
