# M5 moves on to scripted scenes after two clean controls

Status: agreed 2026-10-11 by the owner, who asked for it and set the count at
two, with a third only if something suggests the watch may not be right. No code
changes.

The owner asked, after [control 1](../progress/2026-10-11-m5-control-1.md),
whether M5 could move on once a few controls show nothing significant, instead
of driving ten or more. Three were proposed, on the rule M3's sessions ended on
([trials-are-sized-by-what-they-show.md](trials-are-sized-by-what-they-show.md));
the owner made it two:

- **Two clean controls in a row** -- ordinary runs with nothing moved, the owner
  saying so, and no furniture-sized change in the change watch's log that is not
  a door or something else shown to have moved -- and M5 goes on to its first
  scripted scene.
- **A third when something suggests the watch may not be right**: a change
  logged that cannot be explained either way, the watch refusing or pausing far
  more than in earlier runs, or navigation's pose seen out during a run. A clean
  third is enough; anything else is looked into before the scene.
- **A false alarm restarts the count.** Its cause is found and fixed, and two
  clean controls are needed again on the fix. Control 3's false alarm on
  2026-10-11 ([controls 2 to 4](../progress/2026-10-11-m5-controls-2-to-4.md))
  restarted it on the two-viewpoint fix.
- **Every later run is a control as well**, for everything not moved in it. The
  watch logs every run, so a scripted scene's runs count against the false-alarm
  rate everywhere but at the things moved, and so does any later run. Criterion
  3 is judged on all of them, its rate stated with its interval, rather than on
  a set of dedicated controls driven first.

## Why few dedicated controls

Controls cannot prove a rate as low as the flat needs. The owner's answer on
2026-10-10, that the larger things rarely move unless arranged for a test, means
a change claimed from one revisit is right more often than wrong only if false
alarms are rarer than real changes, which are rare. With no alarm in n controls
the rate is shown to be under about 3/n (95%): ten clean controls show under 26%,
thirty under 10%. Dedicated controls to that depth would be most of a week of
charges and prove little a scripted scene's quiet rooms do not.

What a few controls do show is whether the watch cries wolf on most runs, which
is the failure that would make a scripted scene unreadable. A watch alarming on
half of all runs passes two in a row one time in four; one alarming on one run
in four, a little over half the time. Two catch less than three would, which is
why a third follows anything that looks wrong, and why the false-alarm rate is
judged on every later run rather than on these. They are enough to start scenes
on, not to claim the budget.

## What it changes

M5's criterion 1 asks for controls and scenes "their counts set so that
criterion 3's budget can be told apart from what it rules out". Under this rule
the controls' count is not set in advance: it is two before the first scene and
then whatever every later run adds. Criterion 3's budget is still to be set
before any scripted scene; the owner's answer means it has to come with the
plan's proposal that an apparent change is a reason to look again, not a claim,
which the watch's two-viewpoint rule now begins.

Back-to-back controls compare each room with a visit half an hour old, the easy
case. Longer gaps come from the runs that follow on other days.
